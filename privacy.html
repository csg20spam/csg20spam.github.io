1. Scope, Entity Identification, and Core Architecture

1.1 Operator Identity and Contact Information

1.1.1 Developer Identification: CardStack: Wallet Simplified (hereinafter referred to as "CardStack" or "the App") is developed and operated by StackClub ("we", "us", or "our").
1.1.2 Privacy Contact: For privacy-related inquiries, data rights requests, or regulatory communications, you may contact our designated privacy team at csg20work@gmail.com.

1.2 Core Architectural Principles & Zero-Server Model

1.2.1 Serverless Vault Architecture: We do not operate, host, or maintain any user account system, registration server, cloud database, or remote vault infrastructure for CardStack.
1.2.2 Remote Access Boundaries: We do not collect, receive, transmit, or store your payment card records or personal vault contents on any server we operate. Vault records remain on your device unless you turn on optional Google Drive synchronisation (Section 7.6), which keeps sync files inside your own Google Drive account. We have no access to those files; they are held by Google under your Google account and Google's privacy policy.
1.2.3 External Support Communications: Personal data is received by us only when you voluntarily submit feedback questionnaires, email support, or join public support channels, as detailed in Section 9.
1.2.4 Developer Listing Disclosures: Developer identity details displayed on the Google Play Store listing are managed through the Google Play Console profile.

1.2.5 Optional Cloud Synchronisation: Google Drive synchronisation is optional and off by default. The App works fully offline without a Google account; sync begins only after you sign in and turn it on.

2. Categories of Information Processed and Stored Locally

2.1 User-Entered Data Categories

2.1.1 Payment Card Details: Primary Account Numbers (PAN / card numbers), expiration dates, Card Verification Values (CVV/CVC, optional), and Personal Identification Numbers (PINs, optional).
2.1.2 Issuer and Network Metadata: Issuing bank or financial institution name, card type (credit or debit), payment network (e.g., Visa, Mastercard, RuPay) and variant, custom card nicknames, notes, and card joining dates.
2.1.3 Third-Party Card Records: Payment card details belonging to friends, family members, or business associates, recorded under their respective names. You must obtain explicit authorization from the cardholder before entering third-party card details.

2.2 System Preferences and Application Data

2.2.1 Operational Settings: Custom and built-in web links, reminder preferences, notification schedules, application lock choices, and UI preferences.

2.3 Explicit Data Restrictions and Prohibitions

2.3.1 No Legal Name Field for Primary Cards: The App intentionally provides no dedicated input field for the cardholder's legal name on personal cards.
2.3.2 Prohibition on Authentication Credentials: You must never enter online banking passwords, mobile banking login credentials, or one-time passwords (OTPs) into CardStack.
2.3.3 Non-Payment Utility Status: CardStack functions strictly as an information organizer and digital utility. The App does not authorize, initiate, clear, or execute payment transactions.

3. Storage Mechanics and Cryptographic Security

3.1 Local Encryption Architecture

3.1.1 Vault Encryption Standard: Main vault records are encrypted using Advanced Encryption Standard in Galois/Counter Mode with 256-bit keys (AES-256-GCM).
3.1.2 Cryptographic Key Management: Cryptographic data keys are generated randomly and protected using the Android Keystore system.
3.1.3 Authentication and Key Derivation: Access to the encrypted vault is governed by your selected unlock method:

  1. System Screen Lock / Biometrics: Relies on device screen lock credentials or fingerprint authentication.
  2. Application PIN: Derives cryptographic vault access keys using Password-Based Key Derivation Function 2 with HMAC-SHA256 (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256) utilizing 210,000 iterations, combined with a unique device key.
  3. Unlocked Access: Operates without an application lock if configured by the user.
    3.1.4 Hardware-Backed Cryptography Disclaimer: Hardware-backed key protection depends entirely upon the underlying physical hardware and firmware capabilities of your Android device and is not guaranteed across all device models.

3.2 Unencrypted Metadata and Volatile RAM Processing

3.2.1 Unencrypted Configuration Data: Non-sensitive operational preferences—including application settings, reminder schedules, and display/nickname labels—are stored separately from encrypted card records.
3.2.2 Volatile Memory Handling: While CardStack is actively running, card details are unencrypted in volatile device memory (RAM) to facilitate viewing, copying, and form filling. Certified secure erasure of volatile RAM upon app closure is not guaranteed.

4. Internet Access and Third-Party Components

4.1 Android INTERNET Permission Declaration

4.1.1 Internet Permission: CardStack declares Android's standard INTERNET permission (android.permission.INTERNET). Network access is used only for Google sign-in and the Google Drive sync actions described in this policy, including explicit account-management and cloud-data deletion actions when automatic sync is turned off. Automatic synchronisation of vault data happens only while sync is turned on. Data otherwise leaves your device only through explicit, user-initiated actions (e.g., sharing, copying, exporting, Autofill, or opening web links).
4.1.2 Absence of Advertising and Analytics Code: We include no advertising SDKs, tracking pixels, or third-party behavioral analytics code of our own in CardStack.

4.2 Platform and Transitive Library Disclosures

4.2.1 Google ML Kit Integration: CardStack uses Google ML Kit text recognition to process card images on your device for card scanning. The card-scanning feature does not upload those images to Google's servers for recognition.
4.2.2 Operating System and Service Diagnostics: Your underlying Android operating system, device hardware manufacturer, and Google Play Services may independently collect diagnostic, crash, and performance telemetry under their respective privacy policies.

4.2.3 Google Sign-In and Google Drive: When you enable synchronisation, you sign in with your Google account and the App uses the Google Drive API to create and update sync files in the hidden app-data folder of your Drive. Your Google account and Drive contents are processed by Google under its own privacy policy.

5. Purposes of Processing

5.1 Operational Purpose Declarations

Information entered into or processed by CardStack is used exclusively for the following purposes:

  1. Presenting, organizing, rendering, and filtering card records and metadata.
  2. Executing local reminder schedules, expiry notifications, and alert preferences.
  3. Transferring selected card numbers to the system clipboard upon explicit request.
  4. Formatting text or encrypted files for user-initiated sharing, export, backup, or import operations.
  5. Maintaining user application preferences, lock states, and utility functions.
  6. Keeping, only when you enable sync, an encrypted synchronised copy of your card records in your own Google Drive: each card carries a last-modified timestamp and the latest version is kept, deletions are recorded so they reach your other devices, and expired cards are excluded from the synced card list.

6. Camera and NFC Features

6.1 On-Device Computer Vision (Card Scanning)

6.1.1 Camera Access: When using camera-assisted card scanning, CardStack accesses your device camera.
6.1.2 Google ML Kit Processing: Live camera frames are analyzed on-device in volatile RAM using integrated Google ML Kit text recognition to extract card numbers and expiration dates.
6.1.3 Absence of Image Retention: Live camera frames are processed in volatile memory only. CardStack does not capture, record, store, or upload payment card photographs.
6.1.4 Entry Modes and Vault Commit Rules: Depending on the entry mode selected, detected card details are either presented for review before saving or saved directly when you choose Fast Add. Fast Add mode does not capture or store CVV codes.

6.2 Near Field Communication (NFC) Reading

6.2.1 Contactless Card Tap Interface: When adding a card via NFC, the App reads the card number and expiry date directly from the contactless chip embedded in your physical card.
6.2.2 Scope of NFC Extraction: NFC reading operates exclusively on your explicit request. It cannot read printed CVV codes, read PINs, or initiate payment transactions.
6.2.3 Entry Modes and Vault Commit Rules: Depending on the entry mode selected, detected card details are either presented for review before saving or saved directly when you choose Fast Add. Fast Add mode does not capture or store CVV codes.

7. System Integrations: Clipboard, Sharing, Autofill, and Exports

7.1 Clipboard Operations

7.1.1 Sensitive Content Flag: When you tap to copy a card number, the full number is placed onto the Android system clipboard and marked with Android's sensitive content flag.
7.1.2 Auto-Clear Timer Disclosures: Automatic clipboard clearing after a configured timer is executed on a best-effort basis. Third-party applications or services with active clipboard monitoring permissions may access copied data before automatic clearing occurs.

7.2 User-Initiated Unencrypted Sharing

7.2.1 Plain-Text Share Content: When you initiate a Share action, the full card number, expiration date, issuing bank, and card variant are compiled into unencrypted plain text and sent directly to the destination app or recipient you select, while simultaneously copying the text to the clipboard.
7.2.2 Optional Share Attributes: CVV numbers and payment network details are included in plain-text output only if explicitly enabled in Settings.
7.2.3 Excluded Elements in Shared Text: Card nicknames, notes, owner names, and saved card PINs are never included in plain-text share output.
7.2.4 Recipient Disclosures: Recipients may retain, forward, copy, or screenshot shared text. Plain-text shares do not support access expiration or deletion controls.

7.3 Android Autofill Framework Integration

7.3.1 Pre-Unlock Suggestions: When configured as an Android Autofill service, CardStack inspects input fields provided by Android and may display suggestions containing card nicknames or bank names alongside the last four digits before the vault is unlocked.
7.3.2 Post-Unlock Autofill Scope: Upon vault unlock and explicit selection, CardStack autofills the full card number and expiry date into the requesting app or browser form. CVV codes and saved card PINs are never autofilled.
7.3.3 Destination App Disclaimer: CardStack does not independently verify the identity, legitimacy, or security posture of requesting third-party applications or websites.

7.4 Encrypted Vault Exports and Backups

7.4.1 Encryption Architecture: Export files are encrypted using AES-256-GCM, with keys derived via PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 (210,000 iterations) from a user-supplied export PIN (4 to 8 digits) or passphrase (minimum 8 characters).
7.4.2 Full Backup Scope: A full backup contains card records, custom links, and owner details, but excludes application settings. CVV numbers are included only if explicitly selected. A saved card payment PIN is included only in a full backup if expressly chosen by the user.
7.4.3 Selected-Card Export Scope: A selected-card export includes card details, nicknames, and notes, but strips owner names, custom links, settings, and saved card payment PINs. CVV numbers remain optional.
7.4.4 Passphrase Responsibility: Any individual possessing both an export file and its export PIN/passphrase can decrypt its contents. Exports saved to cloud storage or external messaging platforms are governed by the security practices of those third-party services.

7.5 Encrypted Shared Selection Expiry

7.5.1 Best-Effort Access Date Enforcement: An export of selected cards may carry a sender-configured access date. On or after that date, receiving instances of CardStack hide or remove the imported cards on a best-effort basis, even if the recipient imported them as their own.
7.5.2 Scope and Limitations: This access expiry is separate from a card's printed MM/YY expiration date. CardStack cannot recall exported files, plain-text messages, backups, or external copies, and changes to device clock settings may affect enforcement.

7.6 Google Drive Synchronisation

7.6.1 Opt-In and Default State: Google Drive synchronisation is optional and off by default. Turning it on requires signing in with your Google account.

7.6.2 Sync File Contents: Sync keeps an encrypted file containing all fields of your card records, including CVVs and saved card PINs, plus a small key-recovery file. Expired cards are excluded from the synced card list.

7.6.3 Update Rule: For each card, the version with the latest last-modified timestamp is kept across your devices.

7.6.4 Deletion Records: The sync file keeps a record of each deleted card (its card number and expiry) so deletions apply on every device you sync. This includes a card that had already expired when you deleted it. Records older than 180 days are removed during sync, and Replace Cloud Backup removes them at once.

7.6.5 Expired Cards: Expired cards are excluded from the synced card list. A deletion record for an already-expired card still follows Clause 7.6.4.

7.6.6 Encryption: Sync files are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) and stored in the hidden app-data folder of your own Google Drive; access to them depends on your Google account and Google's access controls.

7.6.7 Storage and Control: Sync files are stored in your own Google Drive, in the hidden app-data folder; we never receive or access them. Turning sync off, disconnecting your Google account, or uninstalling the App does not delete them. You can replace the whole cloud copy from your current device with Replace Cloud Backup, or delete all sync data with "Delete synced data and disconnect", available when sync is turned off and Google Drive holds sync data. The files are also removable under Drive settings, Manage apps (hidden app data).

8. Visual Security, Notifications, and Screen Protection

8.1 Floating Popups and Screen Notifications

8.1.1 Displayed Data Elements: Following a card copy operation, CardStack may display a timed popup or system notification showing the card name, last four digits, expiry date, and any saved CVV.
8.1.2 Lock Screen Masking Behavior: On a locked screen, CardStack requests Android to display generic text, subject to your Android lock-screen and notification settings. Displayed notifications may be visible to nearby individuals depending on your device configuration.

8.2 Screen Capture Prevention (FLAG_SECURE)

8.2.1 Application of FLAG_SECURE: CardStack applies Android's FLAG_SECURE window attribute on sensitive screens—such as those displaying full card numbers, CVVs, PINs, or vault recovery codes—to block system screenshots and screen recording software.
8.2.2 Scope Limitations: Non-sensitive or masked screens (such as the home list showing masked card digits) remain capturable by the system. FLAG_SECURE does not prevent external physical photography of your screen.

11. Data Retention, Local Deletion, and App Reset

11.1 Local Data Lifecycle

11.1.1 Local Retention Scope: Vault data remains on your physical device until explicitly deleted or reset by you, except for the encrypted synchronised copy kept in your own Google Drive when you enable synchronisation (Section 7.6). Native Android cloud backups and device-to-device automatic data transfers are disabled for CardStack data.
11.1.2 Recovery Boundaries: We cannot access, decrypt, or recover lost vault records. Vault recovery codes function solely alongside original device hardware keys and application data, and cannot restore records if app data or Keystore material is lost.

11.1.3 Cloud Sync Retention: If you enable Google Drive synchronisation, the sync files are stored in your Google Drive separately from the local files. Turning sync off or disconnecting your Google account does not delete them, and uninstalling the App cannot remove them. They remain until you delete them in the App or through Google Drive settings, Manage apps.

11.2 Card Deletion and Safety Revisions

11.2.1 Individual Card Deletion: Deleting a card record removes it from the active vault. However, an earlier encrypted vault revision may remain in the local safety copy (previousvault2.bin.bak) until replaced by a subsequent save or purged via Reset.
11.2.2 Forensic Erasure Disclaimer: Local file deletion removes file references but does not constitute certified forensic erasure.

11.2.3 Cloud Deletion Records: When sync is on, a record of a deleted card (its card number and expiry) is kept in the sync file so the deletion reaches your other devices, including a card that had already expired when you deleted it. Records older than 180 days are removed during sync; replacing the cloud backup removes them at once.

11.3 Application Reset Functionality

11.3.1 Reset Operations: Utilizing the Reset option in Settings purges primary vault files, local safety copies, cryptographic keys, recovery records, Autofill suggestion labels, and user preferences.
11.3.2 Uninstallation: Uninstalling CardStack removes all local application data from the device.
11.3.3 External Copy Persistence: Neither deletion, reset, nor uninstallation can remove copies previously exported, shared, or sent to external recipients.

11.3.4 Cloud Sync Deletion: You can delete all cloud sync data in the App with "Delete synced data and disconnect", available when sync is turned off and Google Drive holds sync data. Deleting on one device does not stop another synced device from uploading the cards again; turn sync off on every device first.
